My vision of AI

The next frontier isn't parameter count. It's how long an agent can work, learn and improve on its own.

Horizon over size

Bigger models have diminishing returns. What changes what AI can do is how long an agent can stay on a task: keeping context, recovering from mistakes, and finishing work that takes hours or days rather than seconds.

In practice: my long-horizon model framework.

Agents that improve themselves

The most capable systems will generate their own learning signal. They propose tasks, build the scaffolding to attempt them, and turn the results into training data, so they keep getting better after they ship.

In practice: the self-improvement loop at the core of the framework.

AI that companies can own

Regulated businesses can't send their most sensitive data to someone else's cloud. They need AI running on infrastructure they control. The hardware exists; the software layer that makes it usable is the missing piece.

In practice: Airavat AI, private AI infrastructure for regulated mid-market companies.

Small, open and checkable

Right-sized models can run on hardware you own, open weights and portable code avoid lock-in, and results should come with the evidence to verify them.

In practice: Erawan 1 and Agentic Workflows.

Airavat AI

Micro AI lab

A micro AI lab building private AI infrastructure for regulated mid-market companies in Southeast Asia. The idea is proven enterprise hardware paired with the software layer that turns it into a private AI platform: models, agents and tooling that run entirely on the customer's own infrastructure.

It builds on what I've seen from years of selling infrastructure in the region: many companies want AI but can't adopt it on terms that satisfy their regulators.

Long-horizon model framework

Research · in progress

The design for a small model, 100M–500M parameters, built around a self-improvement loop instead of scale. It's trained in three stages: continued pretraining, mid-training and post-training.

propose tasks→build scaffolds→generate rollouts→learn via RL↺

The bet is that a small model that keeps learning from its own work can outlast a large one that stops improving after training.
